The Ketchup & Copper Discovery

Can Ketchup Clean Copper?

One kitchen accident. One surprising rinse. An experiment worth investigating.

▶ WATCH THE ORIGINAL EXPERIMENT 🧪 TRY THE KETCHUP CHALLENGE
My Unexpected Observation

How It Started

I was making a burger and fries. Before putting ketchup on my burger, I squeezed the first bit into my copper sink because I didn't want the watery ketchup to get on my bread or burger patty.

I accidentally left it there for about 20 minutes.

When I returned and rinsed the ketchup away, the spots where the ketchup had dripped looked clean and shiny. I had not wiped or scrubbed those spots.

That unexpected observation made me curious: could ordinary ketchup help remove tarnish from copper?

Ketchup contains acidic ingredients that may react with some tarnished copper surfaces, but this is based on a single accidental observation — not a controlled scientific study. Results may vary by material, finish, and ketchup brand.

The Ketchup Challenges

Try It Yourself

Pick a challenge, follow the responsible experiment guide below, and share your results with the community.

🪙

The Penny Challenge

Investigate whether ketchup changes the appearance of a tarnished penny. Apply ketchup to a common-circulation penny and photograph Abraham Lincoln's portrait — including his hair and beard — before and after.

Keep your before-and-after photos consistent: same lighting, same angle, same distance.

⚠️ Do not clean collectible coins. U.S. pennies have different compositions across years, and cleaning may damage their surfaces or destroy collector value. Use only common-circulation pennies you do not intend to collect.
💍

The Copper Jewelry Challenge

Test only on items you have confirmed are suitable, solid copper. Plated, lacquered, gemstone-set, antique, or delicate jewelry may be damaged by acidic contact.

⚠️ Check the manufacturer's guidance before testing. If you are unsure of the material or finish, do not experiment on it.
🚰

The Copper Sink Challenge

The original observation occurred on a copper sink. If you have a copper sink, check the manufacturer's care instructions first. Test only a small, inconspicuous area of a confirmed suitable surface.

Some copper sinks have protective coatings or patina finishes that the manufacturer specifically warns against cleaning with acidic substances.

🏆

Copper Keepsakes

Not every copper-colored object is solid copper. Before experimenting, identify the actual material and finish of your item. Many keepsakes are copper-plated, brass, bronze, or coated with lacquer or varnish.

If you are unsure of the material, check with the manufacturer or a metals specialist before testing.

💡

The Cleaning Side-Hustle Idea

Responsible Experiment Guide

How to Test Safely

Follow these steps for a careful, documented experiment. Safety and accuracy matter more than speed.

  1. Identify the Material

    Confirm your item is solid copper (not plated, lacquered, or a copper alloy). Check manufacturer labels, markings, or documentation.

  2. Check Manufacturer Care Instructions

    Read the manufacturer's guidance for your specific item. Some copper items have protective finishes that should not contact acidic substances.

  3. Photograph the Untreated Surface

    Take a clear, well-lit photograph of the area you plan to test. Note the lighting conditions and camera angle so you can replicate them later.

  4. Record What You Use

    Note the ketchup brand and variety. Record the exact contact time. The original observation involved approximately 20 minutes of accidental contact — this is not a scientifically established optimal time.

  5. Apply to a Small Test Area

    Apply a small amount of ketchup only to a suitable, inconspicuous test area. Do not cover the entire surface on your first attempt.

  6. Observe and Rinse

    Observe the result after your chosen contact time. Rinse thoroughly according to the surface manufacturer's guidance. Do not scrub — the original observation involved no scrubbing.

  7. Photograph the Result

    Photograph the tested area under the same lighting and at the same angle as your before photo. Consistent documentation makes your results meaningful.

  8. Record Your Findings

    Did the surface change? Remain the same? Show damage? Be honest in your reporting — negative or unexpected results are just as valuable as positive ones.

Important: Do not mix ketchup with any cleaning chemicals. Do not encourage ingestion of experimental materials. Do not experiment on food-contact surfaces without proper manufacturer guidance. Ketchup contains acidic ingredients that may react with some surfaces — results may vary and are not guaranteed.
